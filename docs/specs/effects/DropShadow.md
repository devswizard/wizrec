# Effect spec: `DropShadow`

A soft, offset copy of the layer's silhouette in a solid colour, drawn behind
the layer.

Tags: **[obs]** observed in files, **[inf]** inferred, **[?]** unknown.
For the shared evidence (E-demo, E-thumb) see `README.md`.

## Parameters

[obs, E-demo: one instance on `ScreenVMFile` 6, Camtasia 2026]:

| Name | Type | Demo value | Meaning / units | Range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `angle` | animatable double | 4.71238898038469 (= 3π/2) | Direction of the shadow offset, **radians, measured counter-clockwise from +x with +y up**; 3π/2 = straight down on screen [obs, below] | [0, 2π) [inf] |
| `offset` | animatable double | 15.0 | Distance of the shadow from the layer, **≈ canvas px** [obs, weak] | [?] |

| `opacity` | animatable double | 0.2 | Shadow alpha multiplier, 0–1 [obs: fitted 0.19] | 0–1 |
| `color-red/-green/-blue` | animatable double | 0.1216 each (= 31/255) | Shadow colour, 0–1, sRGB-encoded [inf] | 0–1 |

| `enabled` | constant int | 1 | 0 means the shadow is off without removing the effect [inf] | 0/1 |

## Observed behaviour (E-thumb)

**Setup.** The screen-recording layer spans canvas x 128.55–1791.45 and the
full height (1080 px, top to bottom). We predicted the background exactly
from our shader fit, then solved each thumbnail pixel outside the layer for
shadow alpha `a` in `out = bg·(1 − a) + shadowColour·a`, with
shadowColour = 0.1216 grey.

1. **The shadow is visible left and right of the layer, symmetric within
   noise** [obs]:

   | Distance outside the edge (canvas px) | Left a | Right a |
   |---|---|---|
   | 4 | 0.066 | 0.071 |
   | 7 | 0.061 | 0.059 |
   | 10 | 0.039 | 0.042 |
   | 13 | 0.030 | 0.032 |
   | 16 | 0.019 | 0.020 |
   | 19 | 0.014 | 0.014 |
   | 22 | 0.007 | 0.007 |
   | 25 | 0.002 | 0.006 |
   | ≥ 28 | 0.000 | 0.000 |

   Left/right symmetry means the offset has **no x component**, so
   `angle` = 3π/2 points straight up or down [obs].
2. **The shadow is weaker near the canvas top and full at the bottom**
   [obs]: a = 0.017 at thumbnail row 2, 0.049 at row 10 and ≈0.06 from row 20
   down to row 358. The shadow is therefore shifted **down**, so 3π/2 means
   down on screen. That fits `direction = (cos angle, sin angle)` in the
   canvas's +y-up frame (`transform.md` §1) [obs].
3. **Model fit.** The shadow is the layer rectangle, shifted by
   `offset` along `angle`, blurred, times `opacity`, and averaged 3×3 to the
   thumbnail. Least squares over 640 pixels:

   | Kernel | Best scale | Opacity | RMS (alpha) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Gaussian | σ = 12.5 | 0.19 | 0.0032 |
   | Tent (triangle) | half-width 30 | 0.19 | 0.0029 |
   | Box | half-width 22 | 0.16 | 0.0037 |

   - Offset fitted with the Gaussian: 15 canvas px (RMS 0.0032), against
     0.0037 at 8 px (= 15 × layer scale) and 0.0050 at 0 [obs, weak].
   - σ 12.5 against 13.75 (= 25 × scale): 0.0032 against 0.0035, which is
     **inconclusive** for the blur's units.
   - The opacity of 0.19–0.20 matches the `opacity` parameter, with
     `color-alpha` 1 [obs].

## Model for implementation

```
dir     = (cos angle, −sin angle)            on a y-down raster
shadowα = opacity · color-alpha · blur_σ( layerAlpha shifted by offset·dir )
σ       = blur / 2                             canvas px, Gaussian [inf]
draw    = shadowColour with shadowα, under the layer
```

- **Silhouette.** The shadow follows the layer's **alpha**, not its bounding
  box [inf; EF1 tests this with M-disc]. It includes the effects earlier in
  the stack, such as rounded corners and masks [inf].
- **Rotation.** Whether the direction is fixed on the canvas or rotates with
  the layer is [?]; the assumption is fixed on the canvas. EF1 tests it.
